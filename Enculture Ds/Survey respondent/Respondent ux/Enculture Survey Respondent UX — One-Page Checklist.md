# Enculture Survey Respondent UX — One-Page Checklist

Use this checklist to quickly validate whether a survey respondent experience
is psychologically safe, unbiased, and completion-friendly.

This is a **review checklist**, not a rulebook.

---

## 1. Emotional Safety & Trust

☐ Does the interface avoid language that feels evaluative or judgmental?  
☐ Are words like “analyze”, “score”, “measure”, “performance” avoided?  
☐ Does the UI avoid making respondents feel watched or monitored?  
☐ Is anonymity communicated clearly without legal or defensive tone?

If any answer is ❌ → trust risk exists.

---

## 2. Mandatory vs Optional Clarity

☐ Is “Required” communicated using text or structure, not danger colors?  
☐ Is “Optional” clearly stated without encouraging skipping?  
☐ Are red/orange/green traffic-signal metaphors avoided?  
☐ Is meaning conveyed through labels, not color alone?

If color implies judgment → bias risk.

---

## 3. Progress & Orientation

☐ Does progress orient the user without creating pressure?  
☐ Is progress shown as “Question X of Y” rather than percentages?  
☐ Are respondents shielded from system or computation indicators?  
☐ Does progress avoid implying speed, performance, or evaluation?

If progress feels like performance → anxiety risk.

---

## 4. Comment Experience (Critical)

☐ Is commenting framed as “adding context” rather than explanation?  
☐ Is the comment truly optional and never blocking progression?  
☐ Does privacy reassurance appear **before or at first intent** to comment?  
☐ Is reassurance short, human, and non-legal?  
☐ Is effort low (small field, auto-expand, no formal submit pressure)?

If reassurance appears too late → drop-off risk.

---

## 5. Language & Microcopy

☐ Is language neutral, human, and non-directive?  
☐ Are “why?” or interrogative phrases avoided?  
☐ Are users given permission-based phrasing (“if you’d like”, “optional”)?  
☐ Does copy reduce pressure rather than add motivation?

If copy pushes behavior → honesty risk.

---

## 6. Visual Hierarchy & Focus

☐ Is only one question visually dominant at a time?  
☐ Are inactive questions clearly de-emphasized, not hidden?  
☐ Is visual noise minimized (no competing highlights)?  
☐ Does the layout support calm scanning and reading?

If attention is fragmented → cognitive load risk.

---

## 7. Motion & Feedback

☐ Are animations subtle and purposeful?  
☐ Is auto-save silent or minimally acknowledged (“Saved”)?  
☐ Are spinners, loaders, or progress bars avoided during answering?  
☐ Does motion reduce friction instead of adding excitement?

If motion draws attention to the system → distraction risk.

---

## 8. What Respondents Should NEVER See

☐ Internal processing (“Analyzing…”, “Processing…”)  
☐ Gamification (badges, points, celebrations)  
☐ Urgency cues (timers, sprint language)  
☐ Evaluation metaphors (scores, success states)

If present → redesign recommended.

---

## Final Gut Check

Ask one question:

> “Would a cautious or anxious respondent feel comfortable answering honestly here?”

If the answer is not an immediate **yes**, something needs softening.

---

### Core Reminder

Respondents should feel:
- Guided, not rushed  
- Safe, not evaluated  
- Supported, not tested
